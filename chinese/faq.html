Scope of Inspection (Managing Expectations)

Our inspections are comprehensive and adhere to the strict Standards of Practice defined by the industry. We visually examine the physical structure and major systems of the home, starting from the roof and working down to the foundation. This includes:

  • Exterior: Siding, grading, driveways, and drainage
  • Roofing: Shingles, flashings, skylights, and chimneys
  • Structure: Foundation, footings, and framing
  • Systems: Electrical panels and wiring, plumbing fixtures and piping, heating and cooling (HVAC) systems, and insulation/ventilation in attics
  • Interior: Doors, windows, floors, walls, and built-in appliances

Old Homes & Toronto-Specific Issues

Yes. This is a critical check for any Toronto home built prior to 1950. Knob and Tube wiring is an obsolete method that lacks a ground wire.

  • Why it matters: Most insurance companies in Ontario will not insure a home with active Knob and Tube, or they will charge a significantly higher premium until it is removed.
  • How we check: We inspect the electrical panel, the basement ceiling, and the attic (where it is often left behind) to identify if this wiring is still active.

Yes. Kitec was a brand of plastic plumbing widely used in the GTA—particularly in condos and townhouses—between 1995 and 2007.

  • The Issue: It has a high failure rate and can lead to sudden, catastrophic bursts and flooding. Many condo boards now mandate its removal.
  • Our Inspection: We know exactly what to look for (typically bright orange or blue piping with specific brass fittings). If we identify Kitec, we will highlight it immediately so you can budget for the necessary replacement.

Vermiculite is a pebble-like insulation often found in the attics of older homes. While not all vermiculite contains asbestos, a specific brand used heavily in Ontario (Zonolite) often does.

  • Visual Identification: We are trained to visually identify vermiculite insulation.
  • Next Steps: If found, we will flag it as a potential safety hazard. We generally recommend treating it as if it contains asbestos unless proven otherwise. If confirmation is needed, we can arrange for sampling and lab testing to determine the asbestos content.

Lead water supply pipes are a concern in Toronto homes built before the mid-1950s. While we cannot see the pipes buried underground, we check the main water service entry point in the basement.

If we see the characteristic dull grey metal and "bulb" shape of a lead pipe entering the home, we will note it in the report, as the City of Toronto has programs in place to help homeowners replace these lines.
